# Canal Commons

## A community-driven renewal network for the UK's canals

Canal Commons is an evidence-first project exploring how communities, technology, commerce and new forms of participation can help protect, maintain and renew Britain's inland waterways.

**Working principle:** make the canals too useful, too visible and too loved to fail.

## What this repository is

This repository is the project's public development record. It is deliberately research-first. We will document:

- the actual condition and funding position of the UK canal network;
- existing organisations, responsibilities and gaps;
- infrastructure, climate and resilience risks;
- opportunities for community participation and fundraising;
- economically viable canal freight and other commercial activity;
- digital tools for mapping, reporting and project transparency;
- possible legal, governance and decentralised participation models.

## What this repository is not

At this stage it is **not** a commitment to:

- replace Canal & River Trust or any navigation authority;
- create a cryptocurrency;
- claim ownership of canal infrastructure;
- operate freight commercially;
- establish a particular legal entity.

Those decisions must follow evidence and feasibility work.

## Initial thesis

The UK waterways are valuable public-benefit infrastructure, but their long-term resilience depends on a complex mix of government funding, charitable income, commercial activity, volunteers and preventative maintenance. The project will test whether a complementary, transparent community network can mobilise additional people, capital, data and economic activity around specific canal needs.

## First research questions

1. What is the current physical and financial state of the network?
2. What is the projected funding requirement and gap through 2037?
3. Which risks are most economically important to address early?
4. Where are existing organisations already solving the problem well?
5. Where are the genuine gaps?
6. What can be demonstrated with £10k, £50k and £250k?
7. Where can commercial activity — especially freight — create recurring canal funding?
8. Can open data and citizen reporting materially improve preventative maintenance?
9. If decentralised technology is useful, what is the least speculative and most legally robust implementation?
