# Community and Governance

Canal Commons is currently an independent open project. It is not yet a finished legal organisation or decentralised autonomous organisation.

## Governance now

The repository owner/maintainers are responsible for:

- protecting the project from abuse;
- maintaining the public roadmap;
- reviewing contributions;
- ensuring evidence and licensing standards are followed;
- documenting major strategic decisions.

## Community participation

Anyone can propose ideas, research tasks, opportunities or technical work. Contributors should not need insider status to make useful contributions.

## Decision-making

For now, strategic decisions remain with the maintainers, but significant changes should be documented publicly and open to challenge.

As participation grows, we can introduce:

- domain stewards;
- working groups;
- contributor recognition;
- transparent decision records;
- community votes for appropriate non-commercial questions;
- a formal advisory group.

## Commercial boundary

Open community governance and commercial execution are different functions. A future commercial entity may hold contracts, employ people, negotiate transactions and manage confidential information while the public project remains open.

No token, DAO or decentralised ownership system is being assumed. Such mechanisms will only be considered if they solve a demonstrated governance, financing or ownership problem better than conventional structures.

## Long-term possibility

If Canal Commons grows into a significant community, we can investigate a legal structure that protects the public mission while allowing contributors and operators to be fairly compensated. Any such structure should be developed with professional legal and tax advice.
