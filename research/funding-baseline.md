# Canal Funding Baseline

**Status:** Initial evidence pass — 28 August 2026

## 1. Current government funding

The current 15-year Defra grant arrangement runs to March 2027. The Government states that the total value of the original arrangement was approximately £738.8m. The annual grant is capped at approximately £52.6m under the current arrangement. [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/defra-accounting-officer-system-statement-2025/defra-accounting-officer-system-statement-2025)

The Government's 2023 review concluded that the grant had demonstrated value for money and that continued public funding would deliver significant public benefits, while also maintaining the policy direction toward reduced dependence on government funding. It announced more than £400m of funding for CRT for 2027–2037. [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/canal-river-trust-grant-review/report-on-review-of-the-grant-agreement-between-defra-and-the-canal-river-trust)

The 2025 Defra system statement gives the later figure as **£401m** for 2027–2037. [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/defra-accounting-officer-system-statement-2025/defra-accounting-officer-system-statement-2025)

## 2. The key financial transition

The important issue is not simply that government funding disappears. It does not.

The issue is that the long-term model deliberately shifts a greater proportion of funding responsibility toward CRT's own income generation and civil society.

CRT's own 2025 reporting described the government grant as £52.6m, or 22.6% of overall income in 2024/25. Boating and mooring contributed £55.2m, or 23.7%. CRT has also stated an ambition to increase fundraising income from roughly £6m to £60m a year over the coming decade. [CRT](https://canalrivertrust.org.uk/boating/boating-news-and-views/the-boaters-update/boater-report-2025/keeping-canals-open-for-navigation)

## 3. Government's rationale

The original policy framework explicitly aimed to reduce government dependence, increase self-sufficiency and move the long-term cost of maintaining the waterways toward civil society. [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/canal-river-trust-grant-review/terms-of-reference)

This makes the central Canal Commons question:

> **Can civil society and new commercial/community mechanisms generate materially more money and capability for waterways without simply shifting costs onto existing users?**

## 4. New resilience funding

In February 2026, Government announced an additional £6.5m for canal repair, maintenance and long-term resilience, citing extreme weather and maintenance-cost pressures. The announcement described the network as roughly 2,000 miles and 200 years old and said the money would help extend the life of critical embankments, culverts and reservoirs. [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/iconic-canals-to-be-better-protected-with-65-million-investment)

This is evidence that the funding challenge is being recognised by Government and is not purely a CRT fundraising narrative.

## 5. Funding questions for the full model

We need to quantify:

1. CRT's total income and expenditure by category for 2024/25 and 2025/26.
2. Investment income and investment asset position.
3. Maintenance/capital expenditure.
4. Fundraising cost versus fundraising income.
5. Government grant trajectory from 2027 to 2037.
6. Inflation-adjusted real-terms trajectory.
7. Estimated additional annual income required to maintain service levels.
8. Which revenue sources have genuine capacity to scale.
9. Which potential new revenue sources create additional value rather than cannibalising CRT income.

## 6. Initial conclusion

There is a genuine strategic opportunity, but we must avoid claiming a single simple 'funding hole' until the accounts and grant schedule are modelled in detail.

The project's first financial model should therefore compare:

- **Base case:** existing CRT strategy and government settlement.
- **Maintenance-preservation case:** additional funding required to maintain current asset/service condition.
- **Resilience case:** additional preventative investment to reduce catastrophic failure risk.
- **Growth case:** additional investment that generates new commercial/community income.
