# UK Canal Network & Governance Baseline

**Status:** Initial evidence pass — 28 August 2026  
**Purpose:** Establish what the project is actually dealing with before proposing an intervention.

## 1. Core finding

The Canal & River Trust (CRT) is an independent charity created in 2012 to take over stewardship of roughly 2,000 miles of waterways in England and Wales from British Waterways. The Government's stated rationale was to give CRT greater freedom to generate commercial and charitable income, attract donations and volunteers, and reduce long-term dependence on government grant. [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/canal-river-trusts-relationship-with-government/canal-river-trusts-relationship-with-government)

This means the project should **not** begin from the assumption that the network has simply been 'privatised and abandoned'. The more accurate framing is a deliberate transfer of long-term funding responsibility from the public sector toward a mixed model of government funding, investment income, commercial income, donations and community participation.

## 2. Governance facts

- CRT was formed in July 2012 as an independent charity.
- It took stewardship of approximately 2,000 miles of waterways in England and Wales.
- The waterways remain subject to a statutory/public-benefit framework and CRT operates as a navigation authority across its network.
- Defra continues to provide grant funding and reviews performance, finances, investment and compliance with grant conditions.
- Other waterways are managed by different navigation authorities, including the Environment Agency and Scottish Canals. Therefore 'UK canal network' must not be treated as one ownership system.

## 3. Strategic implication for Canal Commons

Our initial hypothesis should be **complementarity rather than replacement**.

Potential role:

> Mobilise additional people, capital, technology, data and commercial activity around waterways while working with existing navigation authorities.

This leaves open whether the eventual vehicle is a charity, CIC, cooperative, trading company, foundation, or hybrid.

## 4. Important historical policy objective

The 2012 grant framework explicitly aimed to reduce dependence on government grant, increase self-sufficiency, move the long-term cost of maintaining inland waterways from the public sector toward civil society, increase local/community involvement, and safeguard the network for public benefit. The original policy assessment estimated the long-term maintenance cost of the inland waterways and associated heritage infrastructure at approximately £4bn in nominal net-present-value terms. [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/canal-river-trust-grant-review/terms-of-reference)

This is important because the problem we are investigating is **not new**. It was embedded in the design of the 2012 model.

## 5. Questions still requiring research

- Exact division of responsibilities between CRT, Environment Agency, Scottish Canals and other navigation authorities.
- Legal status of canal land, navigation rights and public access.
- Which infrastructure is CRT-owned versus merely managed.
- How local authorities and private owners interact with the network.
- Existing community-adoption schemes and their measurable outcomes.
- Existing commercial/community organisations that could be partners rather than competitors.

## 6. Initial conclusion

The evidence supports an **ecosystem strategy**. Canal Commons should seek to fill identifiable gaps rather than duplicate the functions of the principal navigation authorities.
