# Failure Case Studies

## Case 001 — Llangollen Canal, Whitchurch, December 2025

### What happened
A major breach occurred at Whitchurch on the Llangollen Canal in December 2025.

### What the interim investigation found
CRT's interim engineering investigation reported that the most likely cause was a combination of a long-term, deep-seated leak beneath the canal bed and the steep-sided, high embankment being predominantly constructed of sand. The leak was described as undetectable to the existing inspection regime. [CRT](https://canalrivertrust.org.uk/news-and-views/news/weve-published-our-initial-findings-on-the-llangollen-canal-breach)

### Why this matters to Canal Commons
The case illustrates the difference between **routine maintenance** and **system resilience**.

A conventional programme can inspect known assets and still miss a latent failure mechanism. Therefore a new organisation could potentially add value by funding experimental monitoring and independent research rather than attempting to replace CRT's engineering teams.

### Questions for investigation
- What was the eventual repair cost?
- How long was the navigation closed?
- What environmental/agricultural consequences occurred?
- What monitoring methods might have detected the underlying leak?
- Were there comparable embankment types elsewhere on the network?
- Could remote sensing or distributed sensors economically screen similar assets?
- What is the cost of false positives versus missed failures?

### Guardrail
This case must not be used to imply negligence by CRT. The interim report states that the leak was not detectable through the inspection regime and that the investigation continued.
