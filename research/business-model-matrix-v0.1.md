# Canal Commons Business Model Matrix v0.1

**Date:** 2026-08-28  
**Status:** Working hypothesis — not an investment recommendation.

## Executive conclusion

The current evidence favours a **commercial infrastructure platform** rather than a conventional canal charity, freight operator, or token-first project. The strongest candidates combine high willingness to pay with measurable canal impact and the ability to scale without owning the whole network.

CRT already operates substantial commercial activities in property, utilities, estates and freight, so Canal Commons must demonstrate genuine additionality. CRT reports more than £60m of winter maintenance and £21m on 22 large reservoirs in 2024/25; more than half of its income now comes from property, investments, utilities, donations and other funding. Government has committed £401m for 2027–2037 while explicitly seeking reduced reliance on government funding. [Sources: CRT Annual Report 2024/25; Defra grant review.]

The leading hypothesis is a three-layer model:
1. **Canal Intelligence** — data/risk/condition intelligence sold to institutional customers.
2. **Canal Projects** — originate, structure and monitor commercially or publicly funded interventions.
3. **Canal Value** — participate in energy, freight, development, environmental and resilience transactions where there is demonstrable additional value.

A token is not part of the core business case at this stage. It must prove utility against ordinary databases, memberships, contracts and financial instruments.

## Scoring method

Each candidate is scored 0–10 on: canal impact 20%; economic potential 15%; identifiable willingness to pay 15%; pilotability 10%; regulatory feasibility 10%; ecosystem compatibility 10%; scalability 5%; defensibility 5%; additionality 5%; founder/operator compensation potential 5%.

Weighted score = sum(score × weight). Confidence is a separate 0–100% judgement reflecting evidence quality.

## Evidence anchors

### Funding
Defra concluded that CRT's grant had demonstrated value for money but that policy should move CRT toward reduced reliance on government funding; Defra committed over £400m from 2027–2037. The 2025 Defra system statement specifies a £401m conditional settlement. [https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/canal-river-trust-grant-review/report-on-review-of-the-grant-agreement-between-defra-and-the-canal-river-trust] [https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/defra-accounting-officer-system-statement-2025/defra-accounting-officer-system-statement-2025]

### Existing commercial activity
CRT reports over £60m winter maintenance and £21m on 22 large reservoirs in 2024/25, and says more than half of income comes from property, investments, utilities, donations and other funding. This means our model must be genuinely additional rather than simply recreating CRT's commercial functions. [https://canalrivertrust.org.uk/news-and-views/news/weve-published-our-annual-report-and-accounts-2024-25]

CRT's property team says it is involved in nearly £12bn of waterside regeneration projects and has a land/property portfolio worth over £500m. [https://canalrivertrust.org.uk/business-and-trade/property-development]

### Energy
CRT says its canals can provide enough latent thermal energy to support heating/cooling for around 250,000 homes and it is already working with multiple commercial and public-sector organisations. [https://canalrivertrust.org.uk/our-cause/threats-to-our-waterways/climate-change/nine-ways-canals-can-fight-climate-change/heating-and-cooling-systems]

### Freight
DfT's Mode Shift Revenue Support and Waterborne Freight Grant schemes were extended to March 2027. Inland-waterway applications are assessed on value for money and require a minimum 2:1 benefit-cost ratio and £5,000 value threshold. [https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/department-for-transport-delivers-more-grant-funding-to-transport-freight-by-rail/mode-shift-revenue-support-and-waterborne-freight-grant-applications-and-background-information]

### Environmental value
CRT estimates combined annual economic and social value of its waterways at £6.1bn, including £1.5bn annual economic value and £4.6bn social value. This is an opportunity-sizing figure, not money we can automatically capture. [https://canalrivertrust.org.uk/about-us/valuing-our-waterways]

England's BNG system makes watercourse biodiversity units economically relevant; statutory biodiversity credits for watercourses are currently priced at £230,000 per credit, but statutory credits are a last resort and project eligibility/additionality must be established rather than assumed. [https://www.gov.uk/guidance/statutory-biodiversity-credit-prices]

## Top 10 and why

1. **Predictive maintenance / monitoring** — highest physical impact and potentially defensible technology/data. The product should sit inside a broader intelligence platform.
2. **Maintenance procurement / shared services** — potentially immediate cost savings and recurring transaction revenue; less glamorous but commercially testable.
3. **Infrastructure intelligence / risk data** — recurring B2B revenue, scalable and low initial capital if a narrow decision product is proven first.
4. **Project origination** — high-margin potential and directly connected to physical outcomes without requiring ownership of all assets.
5. **Water/resilience intelligence** — high physical importance and multiple potential customers; likely a vertical of the intelligence platform.
6. **Energy/heat** — genuine customer economics and large theoretical addressable market, but CRT is already active so our wedge must be additional.
7. **Innovation testbed/procurement** — potentially strong bridge between technology companies and canal infrastructure problems.
8. **Circular materials/logistics** — combines cost reduction, freight and environmental benefits.
9. **Freight exchange** — retains the original freight idea but avoids buying a fleet first.
10. **Development/value capture** — potentially huge but capital/planning intensive and overlaps CRT capability.

## Recommended initial portfolio: three experiments

### A — Canal Intelligence MVP
Build a one-region prototype combining public asset data, open geospatial/satellite data, weather/hydrology, historical incidents and structured project records. The goal is **one paid institutional pilot or signed letter of intent**, not a national platform.

### B — One Commercial Project
Find one real project in heat/cooling, freight, regeneration or maintenance procurement where a third party has money or an economic incentive but the opportunity is not yet properly packaged. Target a transaction with an identifiable path to an origination/success fee.

### C — One Preventative-Maintenance Pilot
Choose one failure mode and test whether additional data/monitoring can detect or quantify risk earlier or cheaper than conventional practice. Measure detection time, inspection cost, prioritisation accuracy or expected-loss reduction.

These three experiments form a flywheel: **data → opportunities/problems → projects → monitoring → measured outcomes → better data → more projects.**

## Rejected for initial launch

**Conventional charity/membership:** useful community layer but weak primary profit engine and overlaps existing fundraising/volunteering.

**Meme coin:** fails willingness-to-pay and additionality tests and introduces significant regulatory/reputation/volatility risk.

**Tokenisation:** potentially useful later for credentials, transparent project records or community participation; not justified as first product.

**Owning boats/assets:** too capital intensive before demand is proven.

**Full property development:** attractive but capital/planning intensive and CRT already has major capability.

## Commercial architecture hypothesis

Long-term revenue should ideally combine:
- recurring B2B data/software subscriptions;
- project origination/development fees;
- transaction/procurement fees;
- selective minority equity or revenue shares where appropriate;
- licensing/API revenue;
- later, carefully structured community/capital products.

A future commercial project could have a contractual **Canal Resilience Contribution**, but this must be negotiated and legally structured rather than treated as an automatic entitlement.

## Founder/operator compensation principle

Canal Commons must be capable of paying founders and staff at market-appropriate rates. Compensation should come from commercial revenue and not depend on unpaid labour. If a model cannot support professional compensation after realistic costs, it should not be the primary business model.

## Critical validations before meaningful capital

1. Interview at least 10 potential institutional buyers of canal intelligence.
2. Identify 20 candidate commercial canal projects and rank transaction readiness.
3. Identify one failure mode for a preventative-monitoring pilot and obtain baseline inspection/failure economics.
4. Quantify maintenance procurement categories where aggregation could plausibly reduce total cost.
5. Map existing CRT/IWA/private-sector offerings so we do not claim false additionality.
6. Obtain legal advice before financial promotion, investment products, insurance activity, environmental credits or tokenisation.
7. Establish what data Canal Commons can legally reuse, license and commercialise.

## Kill criteria

Stop or pivot a candidate if no identifiable payer will engage; customer value cannot be quantified; regulatory barriers make a small pilot impractical; CRT or an existing provider already performs the function substantially better; canal benefit is cosmetic rather than material; gross margin cannot support professional operation; capital requirements are disproportionate to early evidence; or the business depends primarily on speculative token appreciation/donations rather than underlying value.

## Current recommendation

Do **not** choose the final legal structure, launch a token, buy physical assets or raise substantial capital yet.

First prove three things: **someone will pay for better canal intelligence; someone will pay Canal Commons to originate/enable a real project; and additional monitoring can materially improve maintenance decisions.** Those results should determine whether Canal Commons evolves primarily into an infrastructure-intelligence company, a project-development/origination company, a transaction platform, or a hybrid.

The objective is a **profitable, scalable business whose economic activity measurably increases the resilience of Britain's waterways**.
