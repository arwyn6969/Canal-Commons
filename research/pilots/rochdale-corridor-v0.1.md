# Rochdale Corridor Intelligence Pilot v0.1

**Date:** 2026-08-28  
**Status:** Active — evidence-building stage

## Objective

Test whether Canal Commons can turn publicly accessible canal, geospatial, environmental and operational information into a useful **decision-prioritisation product** for a real canal corridor.

This is not an engineering safety assessment and must not be used to certify assets or replace navigation-authority inspections.

## Pilot corridor

**Rochdale Canal — Manchester city centre / Ancoats through the eastern Rochdale approach, with the working study area extending toward Tuel Lane.**

This corridor is deliberately useful for testing because 2026 has produced live evidence of water-management and infrastructure pressure. CRT has reported restricted lock operation following previous water scarcity, and the Lock 81–83 area is undergoing repair after damage to the wash wall, adjacent culvert and canal bed. Current notices say repairs are progressing, with completion anticipated toward the end of October 2026. [1][2]

## Why this corridor

1. Live infrastructure intervention provides a real-world validation target.
2. Water scarcity provides a second risk dimension.
3. Dense urban development provides potential commercial/value-capture opportunities.
4. The corridor is accessible to potential customer and partner organisations.
5. The pilot can remain small enough to execute without major capital expenditure.

## Evidence anchors

- CRT's 2026 water-resources page says major reservoir works are affecting water availability and that reservoir safety/flood-risk obligations take priority. [3]
- CRT's April 2026 Boaters' Update says Locks 1–13 on the Rochdale Canal were given defined operating windows after reflection on the previous summer's water struggles. [1]
- Current public stoppage information records investigation and repair works around Locks 81–83, including the adjacent culvert, canal bed and sunken towpath. [2]
- Government states that CRT has 2,000 miles of waterways and that, at 31 March 2025, 13.77% of principal assets were in poor/bad condition, while only 0.58% of flood-management assets were poor/bad. [4]
- Government has provided a new £401m grant for 2027–2037, reinforcing the need to test complementary sources of value rather than assume a simple funding-collapse narrative. [5]

## Initial data inventory

### Core network / asset data
- CRT open-data catalogue and API.
- Canal geometry and navigation infrastructure.
- Public stoppages/notices.
- Publicly documented works and incidents.

### Environmental / physical context
- Environment Agency flood-risk datasets where relevant.
- UKCEH / government hydrology datasets where licensing permits.
- Ordnance Survey / other permitted terrain data.
- BGS geology datasets where licensing permits.
- Met Office / Environment Agency rainfall and weather data where permitted.
- Public satellite imagery / Copernicus Sentinel data.

### Economic / planning context
- Local planning applications.
- Brownfield / development allocations where public.
- Land-use and employment data.
- Active travel / public-realm datasets where relevant.
- Commercial property/development context.

## Risk/opportunity model v0.1

Each location/opportunity is scored 0–5 on:

1. **Observed/known issue** — strength of direct evidence.
2. **Hazard/exposure** — environmental or structural pressure.
3. **Change signal** — evidence that conditions are changing.
4. **Consequence** — navigation, safety, environmental or economic consequence.
5. **Evidence gap** — value of obtaining better information.
6. **Commercial opportunity** — plausible addressable value.

This produces two separate outputs:

- **Risk priority score** — where additional investigation may be most valuable.
- **Opportunity score** — where Canal Commons might identify/structure a commercial project.

Scores are screening indicators only. They are not engineering condition ratings.

## First hypotheses to test

### H1 — External-data layer adds value
CRT already operates extensive monitoring and asset-management systems. Our differentiation therefore cannot be 'we monitor canals'. It must be cross-system integration, independent analysis, prioritisation, or commercial intelligence.

### H2 — A narrow decision product is commercially useful
The customer should be able to make a better decision because the product exists: inspect, prioritise, procure, invest, develop, insure, finance or intervene.

### H3 — The same data can identify commercial opportunities
A risk/opportunity map should be capable of finding both expenditure needs and revenue-producing opportunities.

## Immediate build sequence

1. Establish exact pilot geometry.
2. Inventory available datasets and licences.
3. Create a source register with access date and provenance.
4. Build a reproducible geospatial evidence table.
5. Identify 10–20 candidate risk/opportunity locations.
6. Manually investigate the highest-ranking five.
7. Produce a two-page decision brief and map.
8. Use the brief in customer discovery.

## Success criteria

The pilot succeeds only if at least one external professional says the output would materially change a real decision and identifies a credible route to payment.

A stronger success case is a paid pilot proposal.

## Kill / pivot criteria

- Data cannot be legally/licensably combined.
- The output is merely a prettier version of existing CRT information.
- No identifiable customer has a budget for the problem.
- The product cannot improve a real decision.
- Engineering validation shows the screening model is misleading.
