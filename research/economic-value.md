# Economic & Social Value Baseline

**Status:** Initial evidence pass — 28 August 2026

## Published estimate

CRT's *Valuing our waterways* work, prepared with Simetrica-Jacobs, estimates the combined annual economic and social value of its waterways at **£6.1bn**. The estimate comprises approximately **£1.5bn annual economic value** from water-based tourism and jobs and **£4.6bn annual social value**, including an estimated **£1.1bn NHS cost saving** associated with active use of waterways and towpaths. CRT says the methodology aligns with HM Treasury Green Book valuation techniques. [CRT](https://canalrivertrust.org.uk/about-us/valuing-our-waterways)

CRT has separately described the network as supporting more than 80,000 jobs and providing benefits to millions of people and businesses. [CRT](https://canalrivertrust.org.uk/support-us/our-campaigns/keep-canals-alive/keep-canals-alive-campaign-update-four)

## How we should use these figures

These figures are useful for demonstrating that canal infrastructure has benefits far beyond navigation. They should **not** be treated as cash revenue available to a new organisation.

Economic/social value is different from:

- taxable revenue
- commercial profit
- government savings
- donations
- investable cash flow.

## Strategic opportunity

The project's economic case should investigate whether a small portion of the wider value can be converted into sustainable funding mechanisms without undermining public access or creating perverse incentives.

Candidate mechanisms include:

- corporate sponsorship
- canalside business partnerships
- freight contributions
- tourism partnerships
- renewable-energy revenue
- water-management services
- events
- voluntary membership
- targeted project fundraising.

## Key research question

> What mechanisms can convert a fraction of the network's demonstrated public/economic value into **additional cash and capability for maintenance**, while preserving the public-benefit character of the waterways?

That question is more useful than simply arguing that the canals are 'worth £6.1bn'.
