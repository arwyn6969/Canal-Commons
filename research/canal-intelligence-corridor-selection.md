# Canal Intelligence MVP — Corridor Selection v0.1

**Status:** Research complete enough to begin a desk-based demonstrator; no physical intervention or engineering conclusion is implied.

## Recommended pilot corridor

**Rochdale Canal: Manchester city centre → Rochdale / Tuel Lane area, with an initial ~20 km analytical window.** Exact GIS endpoints should be fixed when the open-data layers are downloaded.

## Why this corridor

1. **Real infrastructure pressure is observable.** CRT's current winter programme includes canal-bed leak investigations/repairs, grouting to reduce leaks, washwall repairs and paddle repairs on the Rochdale Canal. This gives us contemporary failure/maintenance modes rather than a purely theoretical dataset.
2. **There is a documented failure history.** CRT reported a 2020 emergency response to a damaged embankment and failed culvert at Hebden Bridge on the Rochdale Canal, costing about £250k, plus later dredging costs following flooding. This is outside the proposed Manchester–Rochdale window but demonstrates the corridor's broader hazard profile.
3. **Water-management pressure is current.** In April 2026 CRT introduced restricted opening times for locks 1–13 on the Rochdale Canal after the previous summer's water struggles, explicitly to conserve water.
4. **Urban climate value is unusually well evidenced.** CRT and University of Manchester research found canal water can reduce urban temperatures by up to 1.6°C in a defined corridor during heatwaves. This creates a second value layer for future commercial analysis.
5. **Mixed asset/environmental context.** The corridor combines dense urban development, heritage structures, towpaths, water-management infrastructure and less urban sections. That makes it more useful as a test of a generalised intelligence product than a single-purpose rural asset.
6. **Commercial extension potential.** The corridor intersects dense development and potential heat/cooling demand, creating a future test of whether risk intelligence can feed commercial project origination.

## Important limitation

This selection does **not** mean the Rochdale Canal is objectively the UK's highest-risk canal. It is a deliberately useful test environment. Final selection should be revisited after the national data inventory is completed.

## Primary questions for the MVP

- Can public/open data identify meaningful risk/opportunity differentiation between otherwise similar canal assets?
- Can external data add useful information above CRT's existing asset/inspection systems without attempting to replace them?
- Can the output become a decision product rather than merely a map?
- Can a professional customer use it to prioritise inspection, investment, planning or project development?
