# Canal Infrastructure Risk Baseline

**Status:** Initial evidence pass — 28 August 2026

## 1. Why infrastructure risk matters

The canals are an ageing infrastructure system. Current government policy explicitly identifies extreme weather and maintenance costs as pressures and has allocated additional resilience funding for embankments, culverts and reservoirs. [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/iconic-canals-to-be-better-protected-with-65-million-investment)

CRT's 2025/26 publication data states that principal waterway assets are condition-graded from A (good) to E (very poor), with embankments and culverts included in the principal asset definition. [CRT](https://canalrivertrust.org.uk/about-us/annual-report-and-accounts/publication-data-required-under-the-defra-grant-agreement)

## 2. The key risk hypothesis

A canal can appear operational while containing latent structural or hydraulic failure modes that are difficult or expensive to detect.

That makes **preventative monitoring** a potentially important intervention area for Canal Commons.

## 3. Llangollen breach case study

A major breach occurred at Whitchurch on the Llangollen Canal in December 2025. CRT's interim investigation found that the most likely cause was a combination of a long-term, deep-seated leak beneath the canal bed and a steep-sided, high embankment predominantly constructed of sand. CRT stated that the leak was undetectable to its inspection regime and may have eroded material beneath the canal bed, creating a void and eventual collapse. [CRT](https://canalrivertrust.org.uk/news-and-views/news/weve-published-our-initial-findings-on-the-llangollen-canal-breach)

This case must **not** be presented as evidence that CRT was negligent. The investigation explicitly described the failure mode as undetectable under the existing inspection regime and remained subject to further investigation.

## 4. Research opportunity

The Llangollen case suggests a potentially high-value research question:

> Can low-cost distributed sensing, citizen observations, satellite data, drone surveys, acoustic methods or AI-assisted anomaly detection identify precursors to canal infrastructure failure earlier than conventional inspection alone?

This should become a feasibility study rather than an assumption.

## 5. Candidate risk categories

We should build a structured risk taxonomy covering:

- embankment instability
- seepage and hidden leakage
- culvert failure
- reservoir safety
- lock-gate deterioration
- washwall failure
- bridge/structure deterioration
- towpath drainage and erosion
- water availability
- flood events
- drought and heat
- invasive vegetation
- tree/root-related damage
- vandalism and accidental damage
- navigation infrastructure failure.

## 6. Proposed Canal Health Index

A future index could combine:

**Asset condition + consequence of failure + likelihood + detectability + public use + ecological value + repair cost + climate exposure.**

The index should be transparent and evidence-based, not a proprietary black box.

## 7. Immediate research task

Obtain the underlying CRT condition datasets and published KPI definitions where available, map asset classes and determine whether public data are sufficient to construct a useful pilot index without creating a false impression of engineering certainty.
