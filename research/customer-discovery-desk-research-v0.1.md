# Canal Intelligence — Customer Discovery Desk Research v0.1

**Important:** this is desk research and customer-hypothesis formation, not completed interviews. External interviews are the next validation step.

## Customer 1 — Engineering consultancies / infrastructure contractors

### Likely problem
Tendering, inspection and early-stage engineering decisions require assembling information from multiple sources. Better pre-inspection intelligence could help scope surveys, identify priority locations and reduce wasted site visits.

### What they might pay for
- corridor screening reports
- asset prioritisation
- remote-sensing change detection
- pre-tender intelligence
- recurring monitoring feeds

### Strongest commercial hypothesis
A **pre-inspection intelligence pack** may be easier to sell initially than a full SaaS platform.

### What must be tested
Would an engineering firm trust independent screening data enough to change where it sends engineers or what it investigates?

---

## Customer 2 — Navigation authority / CRT

### Likely problem
CRT already has substantial internal asset, monitoring and inspection capability. It would be wasteful to pitch a replacement system.

### Potential value proposition
An independent cross-source layer that:
- surfaces external signals
- prioritises where existing inspection effort may be supplemented
- links engineering risk with external climate/development context
- creates reproducible evidence trails

CRT's recent response to the Llangollen breach explicitly says it is exploring geophysical surveying technology to improve inspection beneath canal beds. This makes technology relevant, but also means our differentiation must be clear.

### What must be tested
Whether CRT sees value in an external intelligence layer versus building/commissioning internally.

## Customer 3 — Local authorities

### Likely problem
Councils care about flood resilience, active travel, public realm, development, climate adaptation and economic regeneration, but canal infrastructure is often managed by another body.

### Potential product
A **Canal Corridor Resilience & Place Report** combining:
- infrastructure risk
- heat/cooling
- flood/water context
- active travel
- development pipeline
- economic opportunity

This may have a clearer budget owner than a pure engineering-risk product.

---

## Customer 4 — Developers / energy developers

### Likely problem
Canal proximity can create both constraints and opportunities. Heat/cooling from canal water is already a commercial use case, and CRT says canal water could support heating/cooling for around 250,000 homes.

### Potential product
A **Canal Opportunity Screen** identifying sites where:
- heat/cooling demand exists
- canal access is plausible
- planning/development is occurring
- environmental/engineering constraints appear manageable

This could become a lead-generation/origination product rather than a subscription.

---

## Customer 5 — Insurers / specialist risk markets

### Likely problem
Infrastructure and environmental risks increasingly require remote, timely evidence. Government material on insurance and disaster-response data identifies satellite imagery and remote digital assessment as valuable for risk and damage assessment.

### Potential product
Verified infrastructure-risk indicators and post-event change data.

### Major barrier
Insurance-grade data has high standards, liability concerns and long sales cycles. Treat as a later market, not MVP customer.

---

## Customer 6 — Investors / infrastructure finance

### Likely problem
Small canal-related projects are often too fragmented and information-poor to assess efficiently.

### Potential product
A standardised **project data room / opportunity passport** combining technical, planning, environmental, commercial and impact evidence.

This could become powerful if Canal Commons later originates energy, freight, property or resilience projects.

---

## Initial customer priority

1. **Engineering consultancy / contractor** — clearest test of whether intelligence changes a real technical decision.
2. **Local authority / regeneration body** — broader budget pools and climate/place value.
3. **Energy/development partner** — tests the commercial-opportunity side.
4. **CRT/navigation authority** — strategic validation, but likely longer procurement cycle.
5. **Insurer/investor** — potentially valuable later but higher evidence/liability threshold.

## Interview questions

Do not pitch the solution first. Ask:

1. What canal/linear-infrastructure decisions consume the most analyst or engineer time?
2. What information do you repeatedly have to assemble manually?
3. What information would change your decision to inspect, invest, bid or develop?
4. Where do false positives/false negatives create the greatest cost?
5. What external datasets do you already use?
6. What would make you trust a third-party risk signal?
7. Would you rather buy a report, API, dashboard or recurring monitoring service?
8. What budget would this come from?
9. What evidence would you require before paying?
10. What existing product or supplier would we have to displace?

## Commercial validation thresholds

- **Green:** 3+ credible prospects describe a current problem and at least 1 offers a paid pilot.
- **Amber:** strong interest but no budget owner / procurement route identified.
- **Red:** customers say the problem is already solved adequately or no one will pay.

## Current conclusion

There is enough evidence to build a demonstrator, but **not enough evidence to claim product-market fit**. The next real-world step must be customer interviews and a paid-pilot test, not a large software build.
