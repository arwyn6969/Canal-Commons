# Bower Street AI Data Centre — Commercial Screen v0.1

Date: 28 August 2026
Status: PRIORITY TARGET — requires direct diligence

## Why this target matters

A live planning application exists for an AI-ready data centre on a vacant brownfield site immediately beside the Rochdale Canal in East Manchester. The application was validated in August 2026 and remains undecided. The scheme is therefore early enough that unresolved interfaces may still have commercial value.

## Known facts

- Developer: Latos Data Centres.
- Location: Bower Street / Ten Acres Lane, East Manchester, adjacent to Rochdale Canal.
- Planning reference: PLA-2026-001350.
- Proposal: two commercial data-centre buildings with external plant, substations, fencing, landscaping, highway works and ancillary infrastructure.
- Planning documents include flood-risk/drainage, ecology and transport assessments.
- A separate screening-opinion reference OTH-2026-001360 was received 17 August 2026.

## What we must NOT assume

- That the developer needs Canal Commons.
- That canal water will be used for cooling.
- That CRT has an unresolved problem.
- That the project will be approved.
- That there is sufficient thermal/economic value to justify a project.
- That Canal Commons is entitled to any fee without a negotiated mandate.

## Commercial hypotheses

### H1 — Canal cooling / heat interface
Could canal water support a technically and economically useful cooling/heat service? The opportunity only exists if a qualified engineering assessment shows viable temperature, abstraction/discharge, permitting, redundancy and economics.

### H2 — Canal-side logistics
Could construction or operational logistics use the waterway to reduce road movements? This requires route, loading point, vessel, schedule and cost analysis. It should be compared against road, not assumed to be cheaper.

### H3 — Canal/public-realm value
Could the canal interface improve planning outcomes, public realm, ecology or community value in a way that creates measurable project value? Any proposition must be based on a specific planning/commercial need.

### H4 — Stakeholder/interface coordination
If multiple parties have obligations or opportunities around the canal, could an independent specialist coordinate them? This is only commercially interesting if the developer has a budget and a problem that existing consultants are not already solving.

## Diligence sequence

1. Read the complete planning submission.
2. Extract all canal references, water, drainage, ecology, transport and public-realm commitments.
3. Map responsibilities: developer, contractors, consultants, council, CRT, utilities.
4. Identify unresolved items, optional enhancements and duplicated services.
5. Estimate value of solving each item.
6. Identify payer and procurement route.
7. Contact only after a specific proposition exists.

## Decision

Keep as Priority Target #1, but do not pitch a generic Canal Commons service. The first output must be a specific, evidence-backed proposition derived from the planning documents.
