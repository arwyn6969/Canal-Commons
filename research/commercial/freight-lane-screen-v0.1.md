# Freight Lane Screen v0.1

## Objective
Find one real inland-waterway cargo flow where Canal Commons can create value by aggregation, grant optimisation, terminal/interface coordination or customer acquisition.

## Current policy window
The Department for Transport extended Mode Shift Revenue Support and Waterborne Freight Grant arrangements to March 2027. The inland-waterways MSRS route can support eligible bulk/waterways movements where water is more expensive than road and applications are assessed on value for money. The current published overview says applications need a minimum 2:1 BCR and £5,000 value. Funding therefore improves economics but cannot rescue a structurally bad route.

## What we must find
- commodity
- annual tonnage
- origin
- destination
- distance by road
- distance by viable water route
- loading/unloading points
- current road cost
- estimated water cost
- available vessel/operator capacity
- CRT navigation constraints
- transhipment requirement
- frequency
- seasonality
- grant eligibility
- customer willingness to contract

## Initial target commodities
Construction aggregates; waste/recycled materials; biomass; building materials; agricultural bulk; municipal materials; other dense, non-perishable cargoes where loading and unloading can be mechanised.

## Preferred commercial model
Do not own vessels initially. Act as demand aggregator/originator. Potential economics: fixed feasibility fee, successful-route origination fee, brokerage margin, recurring coordination fee, or minority project economics where justified.

## Gate
No route is considered a business opportunity until a real shipper and real operator can be identified and a route-level unit economics model is positive with conservative assumptions.
