# Canal Commons — Transaction Thesis v0.2

Date: 28 August 2026
Status: Commercial validation

## Decision
Do not build Canal Intelligence as the primary business. Test Canal Commons as an independent commercial project-origination and aggregation layer around canal-related opportunities.

## Why
CRT already operates substantial commercial functions across estates, utilities, freight and procurement. Its utilities activity explicitly covers surface-water discharge, apparatus/fibre, water transfer and hydro. Its estates function commercialises property and land. Therefore a generic intermediary selling reports or dashboards has weak differentiation.

## Strongest hypotheses
1. Development-interface services: identify a canal-related constraint/opportunity around a real development and coordinate the commercial solution.
2. Freight aggregation: identify a repeatable cargo lane, aggregate demand, combine with available waterborne support, and earn an origination/brokerage margin without owning vessels.
3. Project-finance packaging: aggregate small canal-resilience/environment/energy projects into financeable programmes and earn development/origination/project-management economics.
4. Utility/energy origination: find sites where canal land/water creates a commercially useful heat, drainage, energy, water-transfer or apparatus opportunity that is not already being pursued.

## Hard anti-duplication rule
A candidate is rejected if CRT can reasonably perform the work internally and there is no clear independent commercial advantage, capacity advantage, financing advantage, aggregation advantage or market-access advantage.

## Required evidence for a transaction
- identifiable project owner
- identifiable paying counterparty
- specific problem or opportunity
- current alternative / incumbent solution
- quantified economics
- Canal Commons contribution that is difficult to replace
- legitimate fee, commission, equity or revenue-share mechanism
- measurable canal/public benefit
- manageable regulatory/charity conflict
- path to paid mandate within 6 months

## Current evidence signals
Government extended MSRS and WFG to March 2027. The inland-waterways MSRS route supports businesses moving bulk/waterways freight where water is more expensive than road, with value-for-money assessment. The 2026 guidance requires a minimum 2:1 BCR and £5,000 threshold. This creates a near-term commercial window but is not itself a business model.

CRT states that freight has a continuing role but that waterways require investment to support additional freight. CRT also operates commercial utilities including surface-water discharge, apparatus/fibre, water transfer and hydro. These facts increase the value of finding gaps around existing activity rather than duplicating it.

## Next execution
A. Screen real development projects against canal-interface opportunities.
B. Identify one freight lane with actual cargo, origin/destination, tonnage and incumbent road cost.
C. Identify one project where multiple funding sources could be assembled.
D. Build a simple unit-economics model for each.
E. Seek a paid mandate before building platform infrastructure.

## Kill conditions
- no identifiable payer
- no economic value beyond information already available to CRT/consultants
- no viable route to contractual authority
- economics depend on speculative token appreciation
- regulatory/charity conflicts make the role impractical
- first transaction cannot plausibly occur within 6–12 months
