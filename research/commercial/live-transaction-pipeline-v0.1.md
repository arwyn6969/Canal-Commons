## Evidence anchors

### Bower Street

Latos Data Centres has submitted planning application PLA-2026-001350 for a two-building AI-ready data centre on a vacant brownfield site beside the Rochdale Canal. The application was validated by Manchester City Council and is awaiting decision. The developer says the location is adjacent to the canal and selected for access to power and transport. The planning record includes flood/drainage, ecology and transport documents.

Sources:
- https://www.latos-mcreast.co.uk/
- https://www.manchester.gov.uk/
- https://plota.co.uk/application/anqr66ou

### Ancoats development cluster

Manchester City Council describes the Poland Street/Ancoats regeneration area as a programme for around 1,500 homes with green infrastructure, walking/cycling routes and multiple development sites adjoining the Rochdale Canal. Jersey Wharf alone provides 190 homes and 6,000 sq ft of commercial space next to the canal.

Sources:
- https://www.manchester.gov.uk/planning-and-regeneration/regeneration/projects-outside-the-city-centre/ancoats
- https://www.manchester.gov.uk/planning-and-regeneration/regeneration/projects-outside-the-city-centre/ancoats/jersey-wharf

### Freight

CRT states there remains a role for freight and that investment is needed to make waterways fit for additional use. Its April 2026 freight toll is 2.08p/tonne-km. Government mode-shift support for inland-waterway freight is extended to March 2027; applications require value for money and the guidance states a minimum BCR of 2:1 for the relevant grant process. The WFG guidance says the scheme expires 31 March 2027 and no new awards can be made after that date under the current scheme.

Sources:
- https://canalrivertrust.org.uk/business-and-trade/freight
- https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/department-for-transport-delivers-more-grant-funding-to-transport-freight-by-rail
- https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/696e3b4262bfa46d3881a273/waterborne_freight_grant_guidance_2026_27.pdf

### Utilities / energy

CRT actively markets utility opportunities and says canal water can support sustainable water-transfer projects. It reports 12 hydro schemes generating approximately 4.7MW. This confirms an active commercial ecosystem, but also means Canal Commons must target gaps rather than duplicate CRT's existing utilities business.

Source:
- https://canalrivertrust.org.uk/business-and-trade/utilities

## Immediate due diligence

### Test A — Bower Street

1. Obtain the full planning application and identify every canal-related interface.
2. Identify the developer, planning team and relevant technical consultants.
3. Determine whether the canal creates an unresolved commercial/technical issue or an unexploited opportunity.
4. Identify whether CRT is already contracted/involved.
5. Only if a genuine gap exists, formulate a specific paid proposition.

Potential propositions to test:
- canal-water heat/cooling feasibility coordination
- canal-side drainage/water-management interface
- canal logistics/construction delivery
- ecology/public-realm value creation
- canal-related stakeholder/project coordination

### Test B — Freight

1. Identify cargoes moving regularly within practical reach of a navigable canal/river corridor.
2. Identify shipper, origin, destination, tonnage and frequency.
3. Obtain road baseline cost and emissions.
4. Obtain water handling, toll, vessel and transhipment costs.
5. Apply available support only as an explicit scenario, not as a permanent assumption.
6. Calculate minimum viable annual tonnage and contribution margin.
7. Determine whether an aggregator/broker can earn a fee without owning vessels.

### Test C — Funding aggregation

1. Identify 10 real canal-related projects with a funding gap.
2. Map eligible public, philanthropic and private capital.
3. Determine whether the project owner can legally/contractually pay a preparation or success fee.
4. Identify one project where aggregation materially improves the chance of delivery.

## Commercial rules

Canal Commons does not get paid merely for producing a report unless the report itself is the contracted product.

Preferred economics:
1. success/origination fee tied to a completed transaction;
2. development fee;
3. project-management fee;
4. recurring service revenue where genuinely required;
5. equity/revenue share only where justified and legally structured.

No token launch, fund launch, boat acquisition or major software build before a real transaction validates the business.

## Gate G2B-1

Pass only if at least one target produces:
- identifiable decision-maker;
- specific unmet problem;
- measurable economic value;
- legitimate payer;
- plausible fee mechanism;
- no material conflict/duplication with CRT;
- path to a paid mandate or transaction within six months.

If all three primary tests fail, stop and redesign the business model.
