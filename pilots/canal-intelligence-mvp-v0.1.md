# Canal Intelligence MVP v0.1 — Execution Plan

## Objective

Build the smallest demonstrator capable of answering:

> Can cross-source public/remote data produce a more useful, explainable prioritisation of canal investigation and commercial opportunity than a simple asset map?

## Pilot corridor

Rochdale Canal, Manchester → Rochdale/Tuel Lane area; approximately 20 km analytical window, exact GIS boundaries to be confirmed.

## Phase 1 — Data assembly

Collect and catalogue:

- canal/asset geography
- terrain/elevation
- geology/soil
- rainfall/weather
- flood/environmental context
- satellite imagery/change indicators
- published incidents and stoppages
- published maintenance works
- planning/development context
- nearby critical infrastructure

Every layer gets source, licence, date and transformation metadata.

## Phase 2 — Manual risk screening

Create 100–250 m analytical segments plus discrete asset records where possible.

Calculate transparent components:

- consequence
- hazard
- evidence gap
- change signal

Do not label outputs safe/unsafe.

## Phase 3 — Opportunity layer

For the same corridor identify:

- heat/cooling opportunities
- freight/logistics nodes
- canalside development opportunities
- maintenance/procurement clusters
- resilience projects

This tests whether the platform can become more than a risk tool.

## Phase 4 — Decision pack

Produce a professional PDF/web-style pack with:

1. corridor overview
2. data provenance
3. priority map
4. top 10 investigation candidates
5. uncertainty/confidence
6. suggested next evidence
7. opportunity map
8. commercial hypotheses
9. limitations

## Phase 5 — Professional validation

Approach at least:

- 3 infrastructure/engineering professionals
- 2 local authority/regeneration professionals
- 2 energy/development professionals
- 1 navigation-authority professional if accessible

Ask the questions in `research/customer-discovery-desk-research-v0.1.md` without leading with our proposed solution.

## Phase 6 — Paid-pilot test

The MVP succeeds commercially only if at least one credible customer is willing to pay for a narrowly defined pilot or equivalent paid discovery.

Target initial paid pilot range: **£5k–£25k**, subject to customer evidence. This is a hypothesis, not a quoted market price.

## Phase 7 — Product decision

### Build SaaS/API if:

- repeated customer problem exists
- data pipeline can be automated
- customers need recurring updates
- gross margin can become attractive

### Stay as high-value reports/project intelligence if:

- customers buy decisions rather than software
- project-specific analysis commands higher fees
- data is too heterogeneous for immediate automation

### Kill/reframe if:

- no customer will pay
- outputs do not change decisions
- existing systems already solve the problem sufficiently
- liability/verification requirements make the economics unattractive

## Budget gates

### £0–£5k
Desk research, open data, manual analysis, prototype maps.

### £5k–£25k
Specialist remote-sensing analysis, technical review and first paid pilot if justified.

### £25k–£100k
Only after customer validation; automate data pipeline and build a production-quality demonstrator.

### >£100k
Requires demonstrated repeatable revenue or committed institutional customer(s).

## Success metrics

### Technical
- ≥80% of output claims traceable to a source
- all inference clearly labelled
- reproducible scoring
- historical incidents can be located and explained

### Customer
- ≥8 discovery conversations
- ≥3 identify a meaningful unmet need
- ≥1 agrees to a paid pilot or equivalent commercial commitment

### Canal impact
- at least one actionable investigation/maintenance prioritisation
- at least one opportunity where better information could plausibly unlock investment or reduce cost

## Non-goals

- replacing CRT inspection/engineering
- declaring assets unsafe
- launching a token
- buying sensors before evidence supports them
- building a national SaaS platform before finding a paying customer
