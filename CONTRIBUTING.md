# Contributing to Canal Commons

Thank you for wanting to help.

Canal Commons is an evidence-led project. We are looking for people who can help turn good ideas into **real, testable canal projects**.

## Start here

If you are new to the project:

1. Read the [README](README.md).
2. Read [Decision Gates](docs/DECISION-GATES.md).
3. Look at the current commercial research.
4. Check the open issues before creating a new one.
5. Bring evidence, not just an idea.

## What makes a useful contribution?

The most valuable contributions currently are:

- a real canal-related opportunity
- a real freight/customer flow
- a potential funder or funding programme
- a developer, operator, engineer, landowner or authority with a relevant problem
- local knowledge that changes our assumptions
- useful public data and its provenance/licence
- a challenge to an existing conclusion
- analysis that helps quantify a real opportunity

## Opportunity template

When opening an issue about an opportunity, please include:

```text
### Problem / opportunity

### Location

### Who owns or controls it?

### Who might pay?

### Estimated value

### Why isn't it already happening?

### What evidence do we have?

### What could Canal Commons uniquely contribute?

### Potential canal / community / environmental benefit

### Sources
```

## Evidence standard

Please distinguish clearly between:

- **Fact** — supported by a source.
- **Inference** — a conclusion drawn from evidence.
- **Hypothesis** — something we need to test.
- **Speculation** — an idea that may be worth exploring but currently lacks evidence.

Don't present a hypothesis as an established fact.

## Commercial discipline

We are explicitly trying to build something financially sustainable.

A proposed business idea should therefore answer:

**Who pays? Why do they pay? How much might they pay? Why can't they simply do this themselves? What makes Canal Commons useful?**

If those questions cannot be answered yet, that's fine — label the idea as a hypothesis and propose the cheapest useful test.

## Issues and branches

Use focused issues. Avoid opening broad issues such as "build the platform" without a defined test.

Prefer:

- `research:` for evidence gathering
- `validation:` for customer/market tests
- `build:` for implementation
- `decision:` for important go/no-go decisions
- `governance:` for project structure

Historical or rejected ideas should be documented rather than repeatedly reopened.

## Code and data

For code, keep work reproducible and document dependencies.

For external datasets, record:

- source
- access date
- licence
- transformation
- limitations

Never publish sensitive personal information or proprietary material without permission.

## The most important rule

**Challenge us.**

If you think Canal Commons is pursuing the wrong opportunity, say so and explain why.

A strong reason to stop doing something is as valuable as a new idea.
